Jensen Huang resigns. NVIDIA enters uncharted territory.

The founder who spent 33 years turning a graphics startup into the world’s AI engine leaves amid an independent investigation and a public personal reckoning.

Jensen Huang has resigned as NVIDIA’s president, chief executive officer, and board member, ending a 33-year run that made him one of the most influential business leaders of the AI era.

What happened

NVIDIA announced Huang’s immediate resignation on November 12. The board appointed Colette Kress, the company’s executive vice president and chief financial officer since 2013, as interim CEO while it searches for a permanent successor. Kress will retain her CFO role during the transition.

The resignation followed a public statement from Huang four days earlier in which he acknowledged that he had been unfaithful to his wife. In the same message to employees, Huang said he supported an independent investigation into national-security concerns raised in news reports.

PRIMARY RECORD “I was unfaithful to my wife.”

That admission is in Huang’s own November 8 message, published by NVIDIA. The statement does not name any other person or corroborate other allegations circulating in the press.

Read Huang’s statement ↗

What is confirmed

  • Huang resigned immediately. The board’s November 12 announcement says he left the presidency, CEO role, and board.
  • Kress is interim CEO. The board says its search will consider internal and external candidates.
  • The board opened a review. Its November 8 statement said independent directors would oversee an investigation after reporting that Huang had been the target of a foreign-intelligence effort.
  • Outside counsel was retained. The independent directors later said they had engaged Joon H. Kim and Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz.
  • Earnings are still scheduled. NVIDIA says fiscal 2027 third-quarter results will be released November 17, followed by a call at 2 p.m. PT.

What is alleged

News organizations have reported on extramarital relationships and alleged foreign-intelligence contact. Those reports are serious, but they are not equivalent to a public finding of fact. The NVIDIA board has not published evidence or conclusions from its investigation.

This file deliberately does not repeat unverified identifying details about people who have not been publicly charged or given a fair opportunity to respond. The relevant public-interest question is whether any relationship created a security, conflict-of-interest, or governance risk—and whether controls failed.

What remains unknown

There is no public conclusion on the scope of the intelligence concern, what information may have been discussed, or whether the board’s investigation preceded or caused Huang’s departure. The board initially expressed confidence in Huang and said it had no reason to believe national security or company secrets had been compromised.

“The Board prioritizes U.S. national and economic security, which is paramount to the interests of NVIDIA and its shareholders.”— NVIDIA Board of Directors, November 8

Those sentences should not be read as either a finding of innocence or a finding of exposure. The investigation remains the authoritative process for answering them.

Why it matters

Huang is inseparable from NVIDIA’s identity. He co-founded the company in 1993, pushed GPUs beyond graphics through CUDA, and became the public face of a company whose accelerated-computing systems sit at the center of the generative-AI buildout.

His exit therefore tests more than one company’s succession plan. It tests whether the AI supply chain can separate an operational roadmap from a founder’s personal and geopolitical risk, and whether NVIDIA’s board can produce a credible account of what it knew and when.

33 years from NVIDIA’s founding to Huang’s resignation

What comes next

Kress’s first public tests include the November 17 results and earnings call, communication with customers and suppliers, and the board’s permanent-CEO search. The independent investigation is running on a separate track with no published completion date.
